# The brief · Contract Sentinel

## The client
**Meridian**, a B2B SaaS company (~$120M ARR). You're meeting the **Deputy GC / Head of Legal Ops**. Her team of two lawyers reviews every inbound contract over $50k.

## The ask (in her words)
> "Every deal over $50k routes through legal for contract review, and we're the bottleneck — sales is furious, deals slip past quarter-end waiting on us. We get ~60 contracts a month, each a 30-page redline from the counterparty, and my two lawyers read them line by line. Half the issues are the same five things every time — liability caps, auto-renewal, payment terms, indemnity, data — but a junior can miss one, or two lawyers hand sales contradictory positions because we don't apply the playbook consistently. Average review is six business days. Leadership keeps asking why we can't 'just use AI' — but if a tool invents a legal position and sales sends it to a customer, we could waive a right we never meant to. That can't happen."

## The metric to move
- **Primary:** contract review turnaround — currently **~6 business days** → target **≤2**.
- **Secondary:** % of positions traceable to the playbook (consistency); lawyer-hours per contract.

## What's NOT in the ask
Nobody asked for an "AI lawyer." The root cause is **inconsistent application of an existing playbook, under volume**. AI belongs on triage-and-flag-and-draft; the legal position stays with a human. Never on anything that leaves the building.

## The built-in tension
"A wrong position sent to a customer waives a right." That fear is the design driver: verification (cite the playbook or say unverified) and a hard block on anything customer-facing are non-negotiable, not nice-to-haves.
